A wide, cinematic view of the Milky Way galaxy seen edge-on from a slight angle above the disk. The galaxy's thin disk is rendered as a bright, warm band of dense starlight (soft golds and pale blues), sharply concentrated near the mid-plane. Surrounding and extending well above and below this luminous disk is a much larger, sparse, diffuse halo-like cloud of faint distortion — not glowing objects, but subtle gravitational lensing ripples, faint starlight bending and dark voids where background stars are warped or occluded, suggesting a vast unseen population hidden within the darkness far from the visible disk. A few of these dark, invisible points are marked only by the way light curves around them (thin arcs of gravitational lensing), never by an accretion disk or glow — they are meant to be felt, not seen. Deep space background, sparse distant stars, no planets or spacecraft. Color palette: warm amber/white for the visible stellar disk, cool deep indigo and near-black for the surrounding volume, with faint teal-white lensing arcs as the only hint of the hidden objects. Mood: vast, quiet, slightly eerie — the sense of an unseen population outnumbering the visible one. Style: realistic space art, painterly but scientifically grounded (similar to ESA/NASA concept illustrations), no text, no UI elements, no accretion-disk clichés.
