# Infographic content brief — Polana-Eulalia Complex (PEC) census

Source paper (primary, for the 4 JWST targets' own numbers): arXiv 2607.16518, Table 1 + Section 1.1–1.2.
External research (family-level census/composition — not in the source paper, which only observed 4 objects): via WebSearch, sources cited inline below.

## Suggested layout

Three-panel infographic, single coherent image, vertical or horizontal strip:

**Panel 1 — "How many are there?"**
- (142) Polana family: **~3,578 members** with proper elements fitting the Walsh et al. (2013) family-defining criteria (Walsh et al. 2013, *Icarus* 225, 283–297, "Introducing the Eulalia and new Polana asteroid families").
- (495) Eulalia family: smaller and younger population than Polana's; **no single well-established total-member count found** in the literature searched — flag this honestly on the graphic (e.g. "точная перепись пока не устоялась") rather than inventing a number. What *is* well-established: its parent body was **100–160 km** across, and the family is **900–1,500 Myr old**, versus Polana's older **>2,000 Myr** (both figures directly from Walsh et al. 2013, matching the source paper's own citation).
- Optional caveat note on the graphic: a broader "Nysa-Polana complex" region has also been counted differently by different methods/eras of literature (e.g. ~2,123 objects via a V-shape identification technique in a separate, later paper) — don't sum this with the 3,578 figure, they're not the same definition/method. If simplicity is preferred, Panel 1 can just show the Polana number as a solid bar/count and mark Eulalia as "population smaller, precise count not settled" rather than forcing a fabricated comparable number.
- Visual: two large stylized counters/dials or a bar pair, Polana filled/solid (~3,578), Eulalia shown with a "~" or dashed/uncertain fill and the age numbers instead of a member count.

**Panel 2 — "What are they made of?"**
- Both families: dark, carbon-rich **C-/B-complex** asteroids (Delbo et al. work on Nysa-Polana taxonomy, per literature search; also directly stated in the source paper's own abstract/intro).
- No member of either family shows the 0.7-μm band diagnostic of Fe-rich phyllosilicates (source paper, citing de León et al. 2016) — i.e. these are iron-poor clay surfaces, not iron-rich ones.
- This paper's own JWST result (primary source, safe to visualize directly): all 4 observed PEC asteroids show a 2.7-μm absorption band diagnostic of **Mg-rich phyllosilicates** — i.e. confirmed hydrated, water-altered clay minerals on the surface.
- Visual: a simple composition tag/icon set — "carbon-rich / dark" + "no iron-rich clay" + "magnesium-rich, water-altered clay (JWST-confirmed)" — not a pie chart with invented percentages, since no clean numeric composition breakdown (e.g. "% C-type vs % X-type") was found sourced specifically to the Polana/Eulalia families themselves.

**Panel 3 — "How big, how dark?"**
Size/albedo comparison, using only primary-source and well-sourced numbers (safe to visualize as a labeled scale bar, largest to smallest):
- (142) Polana (family parent, reference only — not one of this paper's 4 targets): diameter ≈ **55.3 km**, geometric albedo ≈ **0.045** (widely reported value, e.g. NASA/JPL small-body data).
- (495) Eulalia (family parent, Eulalia; this paper's largest target): radius 18.64 ± 0.02 km → diameter ≈ **37.3 km**, albedo p_v = 0.062 ± 0.008 (source paper, Table 1).
- (2441) Hibbs: radius 5.17 ± 1.60 km → diameter ≈ **10.3 km**, albedo p_v = 0.055 ± 0.036 (source paper, Table 1).
- (6769) Brokoff: radius 4.90 ± 0.10 km → diameter ≈ **9.8 km**, albedo p_v = 0.068 ± 0.010 (source paper, Table 1).
- (6712) Hornstein: radius 4.64 ± 0.02 km → diameter ≈ **9.3 km**, albedo p_v = 0.040 ± 0.007 (source paper, Table 1).
- All five are low-albedo (~4–7% reflectance) — visually, render them as dark grey/near-black silhouettes at consistent relative scale, not colored by any invented classification.
- Morphology caveat, honestly flagged: no detailed 3D shape models are established for the three small Polana-family targets (Hibbs, Hornstein, Brokoff) — the source paper's own uncertainty budget only cites *lightcurve amplitude* studies for them (Waszczak et al. 2015; Hanuš et al. 2011), not full shape reconstructions. Don't depict them with an invented irregular/elongated shape beyond a generic rounded rubble-pile silhouette (a safe, generic depiction for small dark asteroids, not a specific claim about *their* measured shape).

## Focal point

The overall story to lead with visually: these are dark, ancient, water-altered fragments — the largest, best-studied members of a population that (per Walsh et al. 2013) numbers in the low thousands for Polana alone, tied by this paper's own JWST data to the same hydrated-clay chemistry seen on sample-return targets Bennu and Ryugu.
